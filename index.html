VITAL Drowsiness Detection Study

Biometric Dashboard — Camera & Wearable Feasibility

1. What this study is about

This is a research study investigating how well camera-based and wearable-based systems can detect changes in drowsiness during the workday. It is part of a Master's thesis at Technische Hochschule Ingolstadt (THI) in cooperation with EDAG Engineering Group.

2. What participation involves

Three sessions on the same day: morning (M1), after lunch (M2), and end of workday (M3). Each session lasts about 10-15 minutes.

  • Wear a Samsung Galaxy Watch on your non-dominant wrist
  • Sit in front of a laptop with a camera facing you
  • Watch a 10-minute highway driving video
  • When you hear a beep, click the on-screen brake pedal as fast as possible
  • Rate your current sleepiness on the KSS scale

3. Data we collect

  • Heart rate and inter-beat intervals from the watch
  • Camera-based drowsiness score, blink rate, head pose, face detection status
  • Your KSS self-ratings throughout the session
  • Your reaction time
  • Context: sleep, caffeine, meal timing, medications

We do NOT store: your name or employee ID alongside sensor data. Participant IDs (P01, P02, ...) are used in all data files.

4. Data storage and transfer

Cross-border transfer: Data collected in India will be transferred to and analyzed in Germany at THI / EDAG for the purpose of this research.

5. Your rights

  • Participation is completely voluntary. It has no effect on your employment.
  • You can withdraw at any time without giving a reason.

Simulation Protocol

  1. A 10-minute highway driving telemetry feed will display.
  2. Audio cues (BEEPS) will trigger randomly.
  3. Upon audio cue, engage the BRAKE PEDAL instantly.
  4. Log your fatigue level using the KSS dashboard at the bottom. Update it immediately if your state shifts.
  5. Ensure the Camera and HRV indicators remain GREEN/BLUE.

Constraints

  • Maintain non-dominant hand (sensor wrist) completely static.
  • Do not anticipate audio cues. React only to the stimulus.
  • No verbal communication or secondary device usage.
