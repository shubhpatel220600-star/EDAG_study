Drowsiness Detection Study

Camera and Wearable-based Feasibility Study — VITAL Project, EDAG India / THI Germany

1. What this study is about

This is a research study investigating how well camera-based and wearable-based systems can detect changes in drowsiness during the workday. It is part of a Master's thesis at Technische Hochschule Ingolstadt (THI) in cooperation with EDAG Engineering Group.

The goal is to check technical feasibility, data quality, and comparability of the two approaches — not to make medical or health assessments.

2. What participation involves

You will complete three sessions on the same day: morning (M1), after lunch (M2), and end of workday (M3). Each session lasts about 40 minutes, matching the session duration used in the research dataset (UL-DD) that this study builds on.

Total time commitment: approximately 2.5-3 hours across the day (including a short intro before the first session).

During each session you will:

  • Wear a Samsung Galaxy Watch on your non-dominant wrist
  • Sit in front of a laptop with a camera facing you
  • Watch a 40-minute highway driving video
  • When you hear a beep, click the on-screen brake pedal as fast as possible
  • Continuously rate your current sleepiness using the KSS buttons visible below the video
  • Answer a few short questions about sleep, meals, and caffeine before each session

3. Data we collect

  • Heart rate and inter-beat intervals from the watch
  • Camera-based drowsiness score, blink rate, blink duration, head pose, face detection status
  • Your KSS self-ratings throughout each session
  • Your reaction time (how fast you click brake after each beep)
  • Context: approximate sleep the night before, caffeine intake, meal timing, medications, wellbeing

We do NOT store: your name, employee ID, or any direct identifier alongside the sensor data. You will be assigned a participant ID (e.g., P01) that is used in all data files.

4. Data storage and transfer

Cross-border transfer: Data collected in India will be transferred to and analyzed in Germany at THI / EDAG for the purpose of this research.

Data will be stored until the completion of the associated thesis research and subsequent scientific publication. Anonymized aggregate results may be retained indefinitely for research reproducibility.

Access to your data is limited to the researcher (Shubh Patel, THI/EDAG) and the thesis supervisor (Prof. Schwerd, THI). Data will not be shared with anyone else without your renewed consent.

5. Your rights

  • Participation is completely voluntary. Choosing not to participate has no effect on your employment or professional standing at EDAG.
  • You can withdraw at any time, before, during, or after a session, without giving any reason.
  • You can request deletion of your data at any time.
  • You can request to see what data has been collected about you.
  • Neither participation nor non-participation will be shared with your line manager or affect any personnel decisions.

6. Health and safety

  • There are no known health risks from wearing the watch or from being observed by the camera.
  • If you feel uncomfortable at any time during the session, tell the researcher and the session will be stopped.
  • Do not participate if you are currently unwell.

7. Karolinska Sleepiness Scale (KSS)

You will use this 9-point scale to rate your current sleepiness. The buttons are always visible below the video during the session — click whichever level best matches how you feel at any moment, especially right after each beep.

1Extremely alert
2Very alert
3Alert
4Rather alert
5Neither alert nor sleepy
6Some signs of sleepiness
7Sleepy, but no difficulty remaining awake
8Sleepy, some effort to keep awake
9Very sleepy, great effort to keep awake, fighting sleep
